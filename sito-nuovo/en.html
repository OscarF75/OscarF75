YOUR NEXT POINT OF VIEW

CONTROL WITHOUT TENSION

BEFORE YOU START / YOUR SETUP

Driving position,
controls and mirrors.

  1. Adjust the seat, backrest and head restraint: reach every pedal with your legs slightly bent.
  2. Keep your hands at 9 and 3, your arms relaxed and your seat belt flat.
  3. Adjust the mirrors and check blind spots directly before changing position.
Read the full explanation＋
  1. Seat and pedals. With your hips against the seat, adjust the distance so your left leg stays slightly bent with the clutch fully pressed. In an automatic, check the same freedom of movement using the brake. You must be able to use the full pedal travel without lifting your hips.
  2. Backrest, steering wheel and seat belt. Keep your shoulders supported and arms relaxed: you should be able to rest your wrists on top of the steering wheel without leaning your torso forward. Set the head restraint at head height and wear the seat belt flat.
  3. Hands at 9 and 3. Hold the sides of the steering wheel at 9 and 3 o’clock. Keep a secure but relaxed grip with bent elbows: sensitivity and control matter more than force.
  4. Mirrors and direct observation. Adjust the interior mirror to see the rear window; set the side mirrors to cover the road on either side, showing only a small part of your vehicle. Blind spots remain: before changing position, also make a brief direct check when safe.
↗ Golden ruleMake all adjustments while parked. A comfortable position must let you reach the controls while staying in control.
× Mistake to avoidSitting too reclined with straight arms, or assuming mirrors and sensors remove every blind spot.

MANUAL CAR PEDAL LAYOUT

BASIC CONTROL / SMALL MOVEMENTS

Pedals and
foot sensitivity.

  1. In a manual car: clutch on the left, brake in the centre and accelerator on the right.
  2. The left foot operates the clutch; the right moves between brake and accelerator. In an automatic, normally use only your right foot.
  3. Move the pedals progressively and test the vehicle’s response in a suitable area with your instructor.
Read the full explanation＋
  1. Identify the pedals. In a manual car, from the left you have clutch, brake and accelerator. Before moving, get familiar with their travel, resistance and spacing; wear stable shoes that let you feel the pedals.
  2. Your left foot has one job. Use the clutch when needed, then move your foot to the footrest. Resting it on the pedal while driving can make the clutch work unnecessarily.
  3. The right foot moves between brake and gas. Make a short, controlled movement, in a V shape where the layout allows. The heel can support light accelerator use and small movements; for hard braking, allow the leg to move too. Don’t lock your heel to the floor.
  4. Move off gently. Add gas progressively as required by the vehicle and slope while managing clutch engagement. In an automatic, normally use only the right foot for brake and accelerator, and check how the car moves when you release the brake.
↗ Golden ruleOperate each control deliberately. Aim for a smooth response without stamping on the pedals.
× Mistake to avoidConfusing pedals, driving with a foot on the clutch or using both feet on brake and accelerator without specific training.

ILLUSTRATION: EACH CAR RESPONDS DIFFERENTLY

COORDINATION / FEEL THE VEHICLE

Clutch, moving off
and smooth stops.

  1. Prepare to move off while stationary, check traffic and find the biting point with your instructor.
  2. Coordinate a little gas with progressive clutch release; once moving steadily, release it fully.
  3. To stop, brake and press the clutch before engine speed drops too low. Timing depends on the situation.
Read the full explanation＋
  1. Find the biting point. In a suitable area with your instructor, press the clutch, select first gear and prepare the accelerator. Release slowly until you hear the engine note change and feel drive begin. This is a zone to feel, not an identical position in every car.
  2. Complete the move-off. After checking space, pedestrians and traffic, coordinate release with a little gas. Once the car moves steadily, release the clutch fully. On a hill, use the vehicle’s specified procedure and any assistance system; don’t hold it still with the clutch.
  3. When to press it. To select a gear before moving off, to change gear and, when stopping, before the engine speed drops low enough to stall. Press fully for gear changes and release progressively.
  4. When to leave it released. For light slowing and normal cornering, keep an appropriate gear without coasting with the clutch pressed. To stop, brake progressively and press the clutch when speed and engine revs require it: there is no fixed number of “last metres”.
↗ Golden ruleBrake with control and disengage the transmission before the engine stalls. Adapt the sequence to the situation.
× Mistake to avoidKeeping the clutch pressed throughout a bend or descent, or holding it halfway for too long: you lose engine braking or cause wear.

EXAMPLE SIX-SPEED PATTERN

TRANSMISSION / THE RIGHT GEAR

Gear changes and
choosing the right ratio.

  1. Study the gear pattern while parked: reverse position and its lockout vary.
  2. To change gear, ease off the gas, press the clutch fully and guide the lever without force.
  3. Match the gear to speed and gradient; select reverse only when completely stopped.
Read the full explanation＋
  1. Learn the pattern while parked. Look at the pattern on the knob: usually 1st–2nd, 3rd–4th and 5th–6th are paired; reverse position and its lockout vary. Some cars have five speeds. Don’t look down at the lever to find a gear while driving.
  2. Moving off and reversing. In initial practice we select first gear while stopped. Reverse must only be selected when fully stationary, following the manufacturer’s procedure. First gear is not mechanically forbidden while moving in every vehicle: any use at walking speed should be learned with your instructor, without forcing it.
  3. Guide the lever. Ease off the gas, press the clutch fully and guide the lever through its path with a relaxed hand. Then release the clutch progressively and reapply gas. Don’t rest your hand on the lever while driving.
  4. Shift up or down as needed. Listen to the engine and consider speed, gradient and required acceleration. Shift up for smooth cruising; shift down in good time for response without over-revving. No single speed or engine-rev rule fits every car.
↗ Golden ruleAlways select reverse while stopped. Fully press the clutch for every gear change and never force it.
× Mistake to avoidShifting into a low gear too early, selecting R while moving or treating “first gear only while stopped” as a mechanical rule for every situation.

ILLUSTRATIVE DIAGRAM: NOT TO SCALE

MANOEUVRES / SPACE AND PRECISION

Parallel and bay parking
and three-point turns.

  1. Check the whole area around the vehicle and move at walking speed, stopping if you lose visibility.
  2. Use references suited to your car: for parallel or bay parking, you can always stop and correct.
  3. Switch between forward and reverse only when stopped; turn around only where permitted and give way.
Read the full explanation＋
  1. Check the space first. Signal, slow down and check all around: pedestrians, cyclists, low obstacles and traffic. Move at walking speed and stop as soon as you lose visibility. Cameras and sensors help but do not replace observation.
  2. S-shaped parallel parking. Pull alongside the reference vehicle with a safe side gap. With your instructor, choose a starting point suited to your car; reverse while steering into the space, check the outer front corner and counter-steer when clearance allows. Then straighten the wheels and centre the car.
  3. L-shaped perpendicular parking. Identify the bay and leave enough manoeuvring space. Reverse and steer using your vehicle’s reference points while checking both sides. Straighten the wheels when parallel to the lines. Seat position, wheelbase and steering change the references: there is no universal exact angle.
  4. Three-point turn. Only where allowed, with adequate visibility and space: move forward steering towards the opposite side; stop fully, check and reverse while counter-steering; stop again, select first and finish aligning. Give way to traffic. Do not do this near junctions, bends or crests.
↗ Golden ruleAn extra correction is better than forcing a manoeuvre. Switch between forward and reverse only after stopping.
× Mistake to avoidMemorising “45 degrees” or a fixed reference for every car, steering without looking or continuing when someone enters the manoeuvring area.

ENTER RIGHT · CIRCULATE ANTICLOCKWISE

REAL TRAFFIC / INFORMED DECISIONS

Roundabouts: read
the traffic and choose a gap.

  1. Slow down, read signs and lanes and be ready to stop: right of way depends on the signs.
  2. Judge speed and distance together; enter to the right only with a safe gap.
  3. Check before changing lanes and signal right to exit. If you cannot exit safely, continue around.
Read the full explanation＋
  1. Approach ready to stop. Reduce speed, select a suitable gear and read signs and lane markings. Signs determine priority; don’t assume every roundabout works the same way. Check crossings and vulnerable road users too.
  2. Judge speed and distance together. A distant vehicle may approach quickly. Keep watching the flow and look for a gap that lets you enter without making others brake or swerve. If approaching traffic is fast or the situation is unclear, wait.
  3. Prepare, then move in smoothly. When the gap is genuinely safe, coordinate gas and clutch to move off smoothly. Preparation reduces hesitation, but does not mean holding the clutch halfway for a long time or rushing to beat approaching vehicles. Don’t occupy half a lane relying on others to react.
  4. Lane choice and exit. Choose your lane according to signs, your exit and traffic. The inner lane is not compulsory at every roundabout. Before changing lanes, check mirrors and blind spots, signal and give way to vehicles already there. Signal right in good time to leave; if you cannot exit safely, continue and replan.
↗ Golden ruleBe ready to move, but make sure the gap is safe. If in doubt, don’t force it.
× Mistake to avoidEntering because someone honks, cutting across from the inner lane to the exit or relying only on another driver’s indicator.

MATCH SPEED · GIVE WAY · KEEP WITHIN LIMITS

LOOK AHEAD / KEEP A MARGIN

Ring roads, motorways
and planned overtaking.

  1. In the acceleration lane, adapt your speed within the limits and merge while giving way.
  2. Keep a safe distance and margin; prepare to overtake with observation and signalling.
  3. Plan your exit early and use the deceleration lane. If you miss it, continue to the next.
Read the full explanation＋
  1. Use the acceleration lane. Observe traffic early and find a gap. Adjust speed within limits and conditions, check mirrors and blind spots, signal and merge while giving way. Do not force your way in or use the emergency lane as an extension.
  2. Look ahead and keep space. Alternate looking far ahead, nearby checks and mirrors. Keep a steady line and increase following distance with speed, rain and reduced visibility. Use the rightmost available lane; don’t fixate on the vehicle ahead or make abrupt steering corrections.
  3. Prepare to overtake. Check that overtaking is permitted and there is enough margin: mirrors, blind spot, indicator and another check before moving. Overtake within the speed limits. Signal and return only with enough distance from the vehicle passed, without cutting in.
  4. Plan your exit. Move into the appropriate lane early, signal and enter the deceleration lane at its start. Normally do most of your slowing there; traffic, queues or hazards may require you to slow earlier. “Brake only inside the lane” is not an absolute rule. If you miss the exit, take the next one.
↗ Golden ruleAn indicator shows intent; it does not give priority. Always find a safe margin before changing lanes.
× Mistake to avoidMerging much slower than traffic without assessing the gap, moving back in too close or braking suddenly to reach an exit.

01 OIL · 02 COOLANT · 03 WASHER · 04 BRAKES

VEHICLE / LEVELS AND WARNING LIGHTS

Under the bonnet.
Safety starts with checks.

  1. Check oil and fluids under the conditions specified in the manual. Check coolant with the engine cold.
  2. Check tyres and cold pressures using the values specified for the vehicle and load.
  3. If a warning light or braking behaviour indicates a problem, stop safely when required and have it checked.
Read the full explanation＋
  1. Engine oil. Park on level ground, switch off and wait as instructed in the manual. If there is a dipstick, remove it, wipe it, reinsert fully and read again: the level must be between MIN and MAX. Some cars use an electronic check. Do not exceed MAX and use only the specified oil.
  2. Coolant and washer fluid. Check coolant with the engine cold, between the reservoir marks. Never open the cap when hot: it is pressurised. Use the manufacturer’s specified product. For washer fluid, identify the windscreen symbol and choose a fluid suited to the season.
  3. Brake fluid. Check the reservoir level as the manual instructs. A low level may indicate wear or a leak: don’t just top it up. If braking or a warning light indicates a problem, stop using the vehicle safely and seek a professional inspection.
  4. Tyres. Measure pressure when cold and use the values specified for the vehicle and load, not the maximum stamped on the sidewall. Check tread, uneven wear, cuts and bulges; a damaged tyre needs inspection before driving again.
  5. Warning lights and initial checks. Watch the ignition self-test and any lights that remain on. Oil pressure, temperature and brakes need immediate attention as the manual specifies; colours alone are not a diagnosis. Stop somewhere safe when required rather than driving on hoping the warning disappears.
↗ Golden ruleIdentify the component before touching it. Use a cold engine and keep the manual at hand for checks that require this.
× Mistake to avoidOpening hot coolant, mixing fluids, overfilling oil or ignoring a persistent red warning light.

PREPARATION IS PART OF DRIVING

CATEGORY B / THE PRACTICAL TEST

Test day. A clear sequence.

  1. Know the test car’s controls and checks: fluids, tyres, lights and warning indicators.
  2. Adjust the seat, head restraint and mirrors, fasten your belt and check the doors are closed.
  3. Before moving, observe, signal and check the blind spot. Pay constant attention to priority and STOP signs.
Read the full explanation＋
  1. Bonnet and fluids. The release and latch vary by model. Identify oil, coolant, brake fluid and screenwash using the manual and symbols, not cap colour. Never open the cooling system when hot.
  2. Tyres and driving position. Car tyre tread must be at least 1.6 mm. Check for damage and the specified cold pressure. Reach every pedal without lifting your hips, with arms and legs slightly bent.
  3. Mirrors and visibility. The interior mirror should show the rear window. Limit the amount of bodywork visible in side mirrors to see the road. Add a brief direct blind-spot check when safe.
  4. A controlled start. Prepare the gear and controls while keeping the car stationary. Use the lights required by conditions, check traffic and signal. Release the parking brake when ready to move safely.
  5. Errors that compromise the test. Dangerous driving can result in failure even without instructor intervention. Failure to give way, ignoring STOP signs, not wearing a belt or inadequate observation put everyone at risk.
↗ Golden ruleShow safe, aware driving: every check has a purpose, including during the test.

POWER: CATEGORY B LICENCES FROM 14 DECEMBER 2024

AFTER THE TEST / THE FIRST THREE YEARS

Your licence is the beginning. Rules for the first years.

  1. For the first three years: at most 100 km/h on motorways and 90 km/h on main extra-urban roads, unless lower limits apply.
  2. For category B licences obtained from 14 December 2024: 75 kW/t and, for M1 passenger cars, also a 105 kW power ceiling for three years.
  3. Zero alcohol and double penalty points for the first three years. Each year without points deductions earns one bonus point, up to three.
Read the full explanation＋
  1. Eligible car. Check the registration and eligibility on the Italian Driver Portal, together with the vehicle documents. Rules also depend on your licence date. Check Article 117 exceptions before driving.
  2. Alcohol and attention. The 0.0 g/l limit applies for three years after a category B licence and also to drivers under 21. Avoid alcohol and phone use: experience and a licence do not prevent distraction.
  3. Penalties and points. Article 117 provides for €165–660 and a 2–8 month suspension for breaching its limits. For alcohol above zero and up to 0.5 g/l, Article 186-bis specifies €168–672, increased in the event of an accident.
  4. If your points reach zero. A licence review tests your technical fitness to drive. Check your balance and follow official notices. Amounts and consequences depend on the offence and applicable rules.
↗ Golden ruleBefore using another car, check that your licence allows it and that the insurance covers you.

ACTIVE COVER BEFORE DRIVING

FIRST CAR / MOTOR LIABILITY COVER

Insurance. Before collecting the keys.

  1. Check whether you qualify for family motor insurance. The same CU class does not guarantee the same price.
  2. Choose a policy that actually allows the intended driver, checking age, licence experience, excesses and recourse clauses.
  3. Before collection, check the exact date and time cover starts and keep the insurance certificate.
Read the full explanation＋
  1. Family insurance and CU class. Family motor insurance extends the Bersani scheme: it may cover new policies or renewals and different vehicle types, subject to conditions. Check household membership, identical residence and claims history with the insurer.
  2. Driver eligibility. Unrestricted-driver cover is not always compulsory. The policy must fit the actual driver. Experienced-driver age and licence requirements vary by insurer. A breach may trigger recovery of costs under the contract.
  3. Recourse and cover options. A waiver of recourse has limits and exclusions: it does not permit traffic offences. Consider driver injury, legal expenses and roadside assistance. Compare actual telematics quotes without assuming guaranteed discounts.
  4. Activation and documents. Prepare the vehicle document, licence, tax code and documents required for family cover. Payment alone does not prove cover has started: check its effective time and certificate, including digital copies.
  5. Driving without cover. Motor liability insurance is compulsory. Lack of cover leads to penalties and vehicle seizure under Article 193. Ask the insurer before driving if the contract is unclear.
↗ Golden ruleCheck driver eligibility, exclusions and start time together: price is only part of the choice.

ILLUSTRATIVE DIAGRAM, NOT TO SCALE

AWARE DRIVING / SEE AND BE SEEN

The space you can see. The risk you anticipate.

  1. Actively look for pedestrians, cyclists and motorcyclists: a small outline can be missed even after a glance.
  2. Leave more space behind large vehicles and avoid their blind spots, especially when they turn.
  3. After a crash, protect yourself from traffic and hazards at the scene. Call 112 for injuries or immediate danger.
Read the full explanation＋
  1. Vulnerable road users. Pedestrians, cyclists and scooter riders have no protective bodywork. Anticipate changes of direction and mistakes. When overtaking cyclists, leave at least 1.5 m where road conditions permit; wait if you cannot pass safely.
  2. Contrast and attention. Rain, shadows and dark clothing can make people harder to recognise. Scan the road, its sides and mirrors. Use lights correctly and never assume someone looking towards you has seen you.
  3. Large vehicles can block your view. Following a lorry closely hides events beyond it. Increase distance to spot signs, crossings and braking earlier. Do not move alongside a vehicle that is turning.
  4. Staying safe at the scene. Stop only where you can do so without adding danger. Use hazard lights, put on a reflective vest before exiting and reach safe shelter if possible. Do not expose yourself to traffic to place a warning triangle or touch fluids, cables or damaged batteries.
  5. Calling emergency services. Tell 112 your location, direction of travel, vehicles involved and injuries. Follow the operator’s instructions. Do not move an injured person unless necessary because of immediate danger.
↗ Golden ruleFirst create space to see and react. In an emergency, your safety makes helping possible.

LESS GRIP, MORE MARGIN

SAME ROAD / DIFFERENT CONDITIONS

The road changes. So should your driving.

  1. Adapt speed and controls to the vehicle, load, tyres and surface. The same journey does not always offer the same grip.
  2. In rain, darkness or fog, increase distance and choose a speed that lets you stop within the visible space.
  3. On water or ice, avoid abrupt steering, braking and acceleration. Act cautiously before grip is lost.
Read the full explanation＋
  1. Vehicle and load. Wheelbase, centre of gravity, tyres and load distribution change handling. An SUV or sports car does not make you invulnerable. Check tyre pressures and secure loads, then take bends at a suitable speed.
  2. Day and night. By day, watch crossings and traffic from the sides. At night, an empty road can mislead: visibility, glare and fatigue reduce your margin. If sleepy, stop somewhere safe and rest.
  3. Rain and aquaplaning. The first rain can make the surface slippery. Water, speed and tyres affect aquaplaning. If steering feels light, stay calm, ease off the accelerator gently and avoid sudden inputs.
  4. Limits during precipitation. General limits fall to 110 km/h on motorways and 90 km/h on main extra-urban roads. New drivers remain subject to the lower 100 km/h motorway limit. Signs and conditions may require much lower speeds.
  5. Ice and fog. Bridges and shaded areas can freeze first. Avoid abrupt downshifts too. In fog use appropriate lights, avoid main beam and use rear fog lights when required, switching them off when no longer needed. A 50 m view does not automatically make 50 km/h safe.
↗ Golden ruleSlow down before the hazardous area and keep room for smooth corrections.

RESPONSIBILITY / THE PHYSICS OF DRIVING

Experience helps. Stopping still takes space.

  1. Total stopping distance is the sum of reaction distance and braking distance.
  2. In one second at 50 km/h you travel about 14 m; at 130 km/h, about 36 m, before braking even starts.
  3. Doubling speed multiplies kinetic energy by four. Under the same conditions, braking distance also grows roughly fourfold.
Read the full explanation＋
  1. Reaction time. One second is a teaching assumption, not a guarantee. Fatigue, distraction and personal factors can delay your response. During that time the vehicle keeps moving.
  2. Responsibility at the wheel. A moving vehicle can seriously harm occupants and other road users. Experience does not remove physical limits: leave a margin for surprises and other people’s mistakes.
  3. The two-second rule. Watch a fixed point: when the vehicle ahead passes it, count two seconds. This is a rough minimum reference in good conditions, not always enough. Increase it in rain, poor visibility, with a load or low grip.
  4. Road surface and tyres. Actual distance depends on grip, gradient, tyres, brakes and load. Wet roads can increase it greatly: there is no universal multiplier for every situation.

BEFORE YOUR LESSON

Private lessons.
The answers you need.

From first contact to choosing a vehicle: useful information for organising a lesson with Oscar Fumagalli.

Ask about lessons ↗
Where can I book private driving lessons starting at home?

The main area is Monza and Brianza. Home pickup lessons are also available in Milan, Lecco and Como, with a quote adjusted for distance. Dedicated days in Bergamo are arranged personally. Enter your city in the lesson planner: we will agree on availability, starting point and time together.

Can I return to driving after years without using a car?

Yes. Refresher lessons are also for licence holders who drive rarely, have not driven for years or feel unsure in traffic. We start from your current level: first vehicle control in a calm setting, then manoeuvres and real situations, with an agreed progression.

Can I learn with manual or automatic transmission and use my own car?

We can work with manual or automatic transmission. The vehicle can be yours or the instructor’s, subject to availability and suitability for the lesson. Tell me your transmission and vehicle preference at first contact so we can plan around the car you will use.

Can I focus on parking, roundabouts or motorways?

Yes. You can request lessons focusing on parking and manoeuvres, roundabouts, urban traffic, ring roads and motorways. The programme is chosen according to your experience and lesson conditions. The website also has eight technical guides for revision while parked, before or after driving.

How much do private driving lessons cost?

Quotes are personalised. The price depends on the lesson type, vehicle used and distance to your home. There is no single price for everyone: send me your city, the programme you are interested in and whether you want to use your own vehicle to receive a tailored proposal.

Which days are lessons available?

Driving lessons take place at weekends: Saturdays from 10:00 to 17:00 and every Sunday from 17:00 to 20:00. Check the calendar for available times. Appointment details and dedicated days in Bergamo are arranged personally with Oscar.

Do you also offer driving theory revision?

Yes. Theory lessons are mainly online in the evening via Microsoft Teams or Zoom, once the planned small group has formed. Individual in-person theory is also available at a dedicated rate. Topics include signs, right of way, road behaviour, vehicle safety and documents.

How do I request a programme for category A, B or C?

Choose the category in the lesson planner and enter your city. In your message, you can add whether you already have a licence or an Italian learner permit, which vehicle you want to use and what you want to practise. Before booking, we agree on a programme and vehicle suited to your situation.
